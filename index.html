ICT324 · Classification research

Predicting road accident severity in Thailand

Which crashes on Thailand's Ministry of Transport road network turn fatal? We classify each incident as fatal, injury, or property damage only, and test how well models find the fatal ones.

    01 The problem

    Detect the crashes that matter most.

    Fatal crashes are 11% of incidents (19,437 of 170,022), so accuracy alone hides missed fatal cases. We report fatal recall and precision, macro-F1 and balanced accuracy.

    01

    Research question

    Which model and imbalance strategy improves fatal-crash detection across three severity classes (fatal, injury, property damage only) while keeping false alarms useful?

    02

    Factors

    Examine the time, location, road, vehicle, and cause fields in the audited files. Casualty counts define the target, so they are kept out of the features.

    03

    Generalisation

    Train on earlier years and validate on later ones, with 2026 kept as a test year and scored once. The same pipeline is replicated on UK road-casualty data (STATS19).

    Related work

      Common finding: tree ensembles lead on tabular crash data, but fatal recall is rarely reported or remains low under random splits. We add a multi-year, three-class benchmark with rolling-origin temporal validation, calibrated thresholds, and a UK STATS19 replication.

          03 Method

          Reproducible analysis workflow

          We follow CRISP-DM. Completed steps are in the progress log.

          01

          Prepare

          Merge yearly files, harmonise names, dates and categories, document missing values, and derive a three-class severity target (fatal, injury, property damage only).

          02

          Compare

          A majority-class baseline, then logistic regression, decision tree, random forest, XGBoost and LightGBM. Each is run plain, with class weights, with prior-adjusted decisions and with SMOTE-NC. XGBoost and LightGBM are also tuned inside each fold.

          03

          Validate

          Four rolling folds: train on earlier years, validate on the next year. Scores are reported as mean ± standard deviation. The model is chosen by a rule fixed beforehand and scored once on the 2026 test year.

          04

          Interpret

          Report fatal-class recall, precision, F1, PR-AUC, macro-F1 and balanced accuracy, then explain errors and important features.

          Validation folds

          Each fold trains only on years before its validation year, so no future data leaks into training.
